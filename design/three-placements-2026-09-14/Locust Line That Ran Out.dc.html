Why it is rightThe two misreadings both came from adjacency, not wording — a permanent fact sitting in the strip where pending things live. Moving it to the head of the turn fixes both without touching the sentence, and it puts the condition before the reply it explains, so the reader meets the caveat before the thing it qualifies. The refinementNear the time marker, not as one. Here is the rule for the three species: a marker that describes the boundary between turns is centred and full-width — time markers, handoff dividers, both of which say “something changed between these two things”. A note that describes the turn you are about to read is left-aligned in the standing register, at the reply's own left edge. Cold start is the second kind: it is not a boundary, it is a property of what follows. What that rules outCentring it, dividing with it, or giving it the uppercase mono treatment the time marker wears. If it currently sits inline with Today 14:02 on the same row, separate them: two facts of different kinds on one line is the defect from §1, in miniature. Memory cardsSame family, same treatment — that reassignment was already on the review list. Once cold start, memory and soft-limit notices all take the left rule, the third species stops being three species and becomes one: a quiet note about the turn beside it.
3 · What a teammate sent Do not build the mirror. Build the attribution.
Yurt sent Open Yurt's conversation
Cursor Read is blind to anything outside the workspace; Shell can see the files. The tree would need pnpm install before anything runs.
The two directions are not symmetric, which is why the mirror felt wrong to build. The reverse sentence exists because nothing arrived — an absence cannot explain itself, so it needs a sentence. Here something did arrive. Presence does not need explaining; it needs attributing. So change one word and add one affordance. Yurt becomes Yurt sent, and the card carries a link to the origin conversation. Together those say “this is a message, chosen and dispatched, and it came from somewhere you can go” — without ever claiming there is more, which is the claim you cannot make without the schema change. It is also always true, so it needs no condition, no inference and no comparison of lengths. Same principle as the receipt ruling: state scope, not absence. And it is not the standing line Colin has twice asked you to remove. Both of those were sentences asserting something might be wrong. This is a label and a link.
Check before you scope the schema change When I read the room code for the exchange ruling, WorkroomSender carried a missionId — I cited it as what makes every room message exactly attributable. If a peer message already carries its sender's mission, the link is free and there is no schema change to weigh. Worth confirming before you price the option you were avoiding. If it genuinely is not there: ship Yurt sent alone. One word, no schema, and it is the half that does the work — Colin's complaint was that he thought he was reading Yurt's reply. He was reading Yurt's message.
