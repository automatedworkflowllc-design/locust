Your four questions
1 · Not under, not interleaved, not folded — the grid becomes the sequence. All three of your options keep the answer grid and find somewhere to put the rest, which means the reader has to merge two shapes in their head to recover one conversation. A grid is a claim: these arrived in parallel and none is a reply to another. That claim is true for the independent-answers case and false the instant an argument starts — so the honest move is not to house the exchange, it is to stop making the claim. And a fold is wrong for a second reason: a fold means “work, summarised”. This is not work, it is what they said. Said does not collapse — that is the register rule you already enforce. Consecutive messages from one speaker group under a single face, which matters here more than in a normal thread: in a fits-and-starts argument Gem will often say two things while Wren is busy, and drawing that as two speakers is a lie about the rhythm.
2 · No — and the worry dissolves rather than being solved. You are right that a card showing a member's first answer above four better ones is showing the least interesting thing they said. But once the post is a sequence there is no first-answer card to fix: the first answer is simply the first item, and it earns its position by being first rather than by being framed. A card is Standing in your register test, and a teammate talking is Said. The answer cards have always been slightly the wrong register; they got away with it while each post produced exactly one utterance per member, because a one-utterance sequence and a card look the same. The argument is what exposed it. Keep the card for the no-exchange case anyway. A grid of three peers genuinely is a different object from a conversation, and it reads better as cards than as three lonely speakers.
3 · Two voices, and they are not in competition — one is present tense, one is past. Follow ROOM-AT-SCALE exactly for waiting for a slot: it is roster state, it resolves itself, and it belongs in the composer note — Wren is finishing another mission. Their reply is queued. Not amber: nothing is wrong and nobody needs to do anything. But an exchange that ended is a fact about that post, not about the room now — and if you put it in the composer it will be wrong the moment someone scrolls to an older post that stopped for a different reason. So it goes at the foot of its own exchange, in the standing register, once. That is not a second status voice; it is the same split the app already makes between a live mission header and the receipt at the bottom of the thread. Ended without a reply is the exception and it is per-message, not per-exchange. It belongs in the sequence, in that teammate's slot, attributed to them. In a sequence, absence is information — if you drop the silent turn, the reader sees the argument stop and blames the budget. Draw the gap or they will infer the wrong cause. None of the three is amber. Out of budget is the configured number doing exactly what it was set to do; a rule working as intended is not an alert. It gets a muted rule and a pointer at the composer, the same pattern as the Plan-mode note.
4 · Yes — but as the foot of the exchange, not a strip, and with the names removed. In a mission thread the strip is a header because the whole thread is one participant's slice of the exchange. In a room an exchange belongs to a post, there can be several posts, and the facts it carries are about something that has already happened — so it sits under the thing it describes. Drop Gem · Wren from it. In the room the participants are the speakers, on screen, with their faces beside every message — naming them again in a summary line underneath is the app repeating itself, which is the note I keep writing. Keep the budget and the cost, which nothing else says. Merge it with the ending: the count and the reason are adjacent facts and two lines saying adjacent facts in the same place is one line. While the argument is live the same line reads 4 of 6 automatic replies with no reason attached, which doubles as the runway indicator.
Two things you did not ask, and one is a shipping risk A late message under an older post will look like a bug. If Colin posts twice while an argument from the first post is still running, a reply lands under the older post — above a newer one — and the room appears to have inserted a message into the past. It has not; the record is right. But it needs the disambiguator: a message that arrives after a later post exists shows its time, and no other message needs to. That is the one case where a timestamp is load-bearing rather than decoration. Worth saying plainly: this makes the room a group chat, and that is correct. The grid was the anomaly — justified for parallel answers and nothing else. Everything you already have supports it: exchangeOf gives you the sequence, WorkroomSender.missionId attributes every message exactly, and RoomPost.missions assigns each one to its post. Nothing here is invented; the data has been sitting in the wrong shape.
